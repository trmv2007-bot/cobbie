# What a player can do, and what Cobbie still cannot

Rishi's target for Cobbie is "everything a player can do in Minecraft". This document is the
inventory of that target: about 150 discrete capabilities, each with the exact game API that performs
it, whether Cobbie has it today (with a file and line), what implementing it actually needs, and
whether a companion needs it at all.

Surveyed read-only against the real 26.3 jar
(`~/.gradle/caches/fabric-loom/minecraftMaven/net/minecraft/minecraft-merged-deobf/26.3/`) with
`javap -p` and `python zipfile`, and against `src/main/java/com/rishi/cobbie` at 0.18.1. Every class
and method named below was confirmed present in that jar; a name that does not exist is called out
rather than guessed.

## The three things that actually block parity

Four independent surveys of different areas arrived at the same three blockers. They are structural,
not a longer verb list, and each one gates a whole family of capabilities:

1. **Every "do it like a player" entry point takes a `Player`.** `ItemStack.use`,
   `ItemStack.useOn(UseOnContext)`, `ItemStack.interactLivingEntity`, `ServerPlayerGameMode.useItemOn`
   and `useItem`, `BlockStateBase.useItemOn`/`useWithoutItem` (what chests, levers, buttons, note blocks
   and signs implement), `Player.interactOn` → `mobInteract` (shear, milk, feed, tame, trade),
   `Player.openMenu`, `Player.startSleepInBed`, `Player.getFoodData`, `Player.experienceLevel`. Cobbie
   is a `PathfinderMob`, so all of it is closed to him. The way through is a synthetic, never-spawned
   `ServerPlayer` — a "fake player" — used as the *actor* for these calls while Cobbie's own entity
   remains the body. This is the single most important piece of work on the list.
2. **He never touches a `BlockEntity`.** `grep BlockEntity src/main/java` returns nothing. Furnace,
   chest, barrel, shulker box, brewing stand, stonecutter, anvil and enchanting table are all block
   entities, so no station exists in his capability surface. This one needs **no** `Player`: reading and
   writing a `Container` is plain `getBlockEntity` + `Container.setItem`/`removeItem`, and the
   machines tick themselves. It is the cheapest real capability in the whole document, and it is what
   unblocks iron (a furnace) and therefore every tool above stone.
3. **Nine slots is not an inventory.** `CobbieConfig.inventory_size` defaults to 9 inside a flat
   `SimpleContainer` (`CobbieEntity.java:160`, `CobbieConfig.java:144`) — no hotbar, no selected slot,
   no offhand, and `Inventory` cannot even be constructed without a `Player`. A mining run overflows
   to the ground and failed piles are blacklisted for 30 s. Parity needs 36 + 9 + armour + offhand,
   which is a save-format migration (slots are persisted as one `ITEM_LIST`; `CobbieEntity.java:2158`).

## The plan, in the order it will be built

Each phase is its own pull request with in-game evidence, the way 0.18.1 shipped.

| Phase | Theme | What lands | Blocked on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hands** | Correct block placement through `BlockPlaceContext` (stairs, slabs, doors, beds, signs, torches, chest facing — his `placeBlock` currently uses `defaultBlockState()` and only fixes horizontal facing); container read/write for chest, barrel, shulker and furnace; smelting folded into the craft chain so iron is reachable; doors planned through and opened (`setCanOpenDoors` + `OpenDoorGoal`); navigator moved to `AmphibiousPathNavigation` plus `setCanPathToTargetsBelowSurface`, `STEP_HEIGHT`, and malus pricing for lava, fire and open trapdoors; attack speed via `Attributes.ATTACK_SPEED` with real charge timing, and a fight that continues until the target is dead | nothing |
| 2 | **Exactly what a player does** | The fake-player actor, and with it one `use` verb that dispatches by held item: bows, crossbows, potions, buckets, seeds, bone meal, flint and steel, shears, fishing rod, milk; entity interaction (shear, milk, feed, breed, tame, name); and real station menus — crafting table with the special recipes, anvil, enchanting table, brewing stand, stonecutter, smithing table. Plus XP and levels, which is what makes enchanting, anvil repair and mending mean anything | phase 1 (inventory, because menus need one) |
| 3 | **Travelling** | Boats, minecarts, ridden animals, elytra with fireworks, ladders and vines, sleeping in a bed, and following the owner through a nether or end portal (today `owner()` only sees the same dimension, so he parks at the portal) | phase 2 (riding is `Player`-input-shaped) |
| 4 | **Living in the world** | Farming (till, plant, bone meal, harvest), animals (breeding, taming, leads, shearing, milking), beehives and fishing, redstone (levers, buttons, pressure plates, pistons), signs and item frames, villager trading, beacon pyramids, and the long tail of decorative blocks | phase 2 (all of it is item use) |

## What this means for "everything a player can do"

It is reachable, and roughly two-thirds of the gap closes with the two structural pieces (the fake
player and a real inventory). The other third is content breadth — every station, every tool, every
interaction — which is mechanical once those pieces exist, and which grows in the phases above.

Three capabilities will stay deliberately different rather than absent, and the reason matters:

- **Hunger — no longer a difference (0.85.0).** Rishi decided he should eat and drink as a player does.
  He carries the game's own `FoodData`, saved by `FoodData`'s own methods, and `FoodData.tick` runs on a
  phantom actor whose food bar, health and effects are his (`Hunger.Actor`). Mobs still do not starve;
  he is no longer just a mob.
- **Sleeping and respawning.** `Player.startSleepInBed` and `setRespawnPosition` are player-only, and the
  result is evaluated against a player's respawn anchor. He can stand by the bed; he cannot set your
  spawn.
- **Death.** He already hands his whole bag and armour to you on death rather than dropping it
  (`CobbieEntity.java:691`), which is better than a player's behaviour and will stay.

## Area 1 — combat, defence, effects

# Cobbie — combat, defence and effects (Minecraft 26.3 / Mojang names)

Read-only survey against `minecraft-merged-deobf-26.3.jar` with `javap -p`, plus `src/main/java/com/rishi/cobbie`.
Everything named below exists in that jar under that exact name; anything I could not find is called out as missing.

Three shape changes in 26.3 matter before any of this is implementable:

- **There is no `SwordItem`.** `javap net.minecraft.world.item.SwordItem` → class not found. Gear behaviour is data components: `DataComponents.WEAPON` (`world.item.component.Weapon`), `DataComponents.BLOCKS_ATTACKS` (`world.item.component.BlocksAttacks`), `DataComponents.ATTACK_RANGE` (`world.item.component.AttackRange`), `DataComponents.KINETIC_WEAPON`, `DataComponents.PIERCING_WEAPON`, `DataComponents.ATTRIBUTE_MODIFIERS`. Swords are plain `Items.IRON_SWORD` etc. carrying those components.
- **`BlocksAttacks` is the shield.** `ShieldItem` is only a constructor and `getName`; the block behaviour lives in the component (`BlocksAttacks.resolveBlockedDamage(DamageSource, float, double)`, `onBlocked(ServerLevel, LivingEntity)`, `hurtBlockingItem(...)`) and is consumed by `LivingEntity.applyItemBlocking(ServerLevel, DamageSource, float)`, which `hurtServer` calls. So any `LivingEntity` that is `isUsingItem()` with a `BLOCKS_ATTACKS` item blocks — Cobbie included, at zero new code once he starts using a shield.
- **`Mob.doHurtTarget(ServerLevel, Entity)` is a real player-ish damage pipeline.** Its bytecode calls, in order: `getAttributeValue(Attributes.ATTACK_DAMAGE)`, `getWeaponItem()`, `stack.getDamageSource(this)`, `EnchantmentHelper.modifyDamage(ServerLevel, ItemStack, Entity, DamageSource, float)`, `Item.getAttackDamageBonus(...)`, target `hurtServer`, `getKnockback(...)` + `causeExtraKnockback(...)`, `stack.hurtEnemy(victim, attacker)`, `EnchantmentHelper.doPostAttackEffects(...)`, `setLastHurtMob`, `playAttackSound`, `postPiercingAttack`. Sharpness/Smite/fire aspect/knockback/mace-smash/unbreaking-on-hit and armour durability therefore already work for Cobbie with no code. What it does **not** do is crit, sweep, attack-strength scaling, or attack-speed timing (those live in `Player.attack(Entity)`, which is unreachable because `Player` is a different class from `PathfinderMob`).

## What the mod currently gets wrong or omits

`attackTick` finalises the action after **one** successful hit (`CobbieEntity.java:1899-1903`): a "kill that zombie" step ends the moment the first swing lands, so anything with 20 HP survives and the model has to re-plan. The `MeleeAttackGoal` registered at line 231 fights alongside `attackTick`, so two damage sources can share one target — the goal exists for the defend-the-owner case, but nothing stops both paths swinging in the same tick window.

The cooldown is a flat 16 ticks, so a sword (1.6 attack speed, ~12.5 ticks) swings slower than vanilla and an axe (1.0, 20 ticks) swings faster, which is exactly backwards from how a player feels the weapons.

Combat permission is two flags only — `combat_allowed`, `player_combat_allowed` (`CobbieConfig.java:125-126`, `383-384`) — so there is no per-capability gate for "may throw potions" or "may block", and `permits("attack")` maps to the one flag (`CobbieConfig.java:437`). Whatever new verbs arrive need their own arms there, or they inherit the wrong switch.

Finally, he has no concept of gear quality: `equipFromBag` (`1374-1409`) equips literally what the string names, never the best item, and nothing re-equips after death, so a companion who dies in a fight comes back naked with his armour in the owner's inventory (`691-719`).

## Verified absences worth not re-guessing

`SwordItem`, `EnchantmentHelper.getSweepingDamageRatio`, `LivingEntity.getArmorCoverPercentage`, and any public `Player`-equivalent crit/sweep entry point do not exist under those names in this jar. `EntityType` is `EntityTypes` here (`EntityTypes.SNOWBALL`, `EntityTypes.SPLASH_POTION`, …). `Items` field names (`TOTEM_OF_UNDYING`, `SHIELD`, `BOW`, `CROSSBOW`, `TRIDENT`, `MACE`, `ENDER_PEARL`, `SNOWBALL`, `EGG`, `ARROW`, `SPECTRAL_ARROW`, `TIPPED_ARROW`, `POTION`, `SPLASH_POTION`, `LINGERING_POTION`, `FISHING_ROD`) all exist, but swords are plain `Item`s with components, not a `SwordItem` class.

# Cobbie — inventory, crafting stations and progression

Read-only survey against the authoritative jar
(`~/.gradle/caches/fabric-loom/minecraftMaven/net/minecraft/minecraft-merged-deobf/26.3/minecraft-merged-deobf-26.3.jar`,
inspected with `javap -p` and `python zipfile`) and the mod source at
`src/main/java/com/rishi/cobbie`. Every class/method named below was confirmed present in that jar;
every Cobbie line number was confirmed by grep.

Two structural facts drive most of the table:

1. **Cobbie is a `PathfinderMob`, not a `Player`.** The whole "use an item / use a block / use an entity"
   dispatch path in 26.3 is `Player`-typed: `ItemStack.use(Level, Player, InteractionHand)`,
   `ItemStack.useOn(UseOnContext)` (whose ctor takes a `Player`), `ItemStack.interactLivingEntity(Player, LivingEntity, InteractionHand)`,
   `ServerPlayerGameMode.useItemOn(ServerPlayer, Level, ItemStack, InteractionHand, BlockHitResult)` and
   `ServerPlayerGameMode.useItem(ServerPlayer, Level, ItemStack, InteractionHand)`. `BlockState` has no
   `useItemOn`/`useWithoutItem` at all any more — they live `protected` on `BlockBehaviour`, so
   `BlockBehaviour.useItemOn(ItemStack, BlockState, Level, BlockPos, Player, InteractionHand, BlockHitResult)`
   is not callable from outside either. Any "act like a player" capability therefore needs either a
   synthetic `ServerPlayer`/`FakePlayer` or a hand-rolled reimplementation per behaviour.
2. **Cobbie never touches a `BlockEntity`.** `grep -rn "BlockEntity" src/main/java/com/rishi/cobbie` outside
   `CobbieEntity` returns nothing and `CobbieEntity` has no reference either. Furnace, brewing stand,
   stonecutter, anvil, enchanting table, chest and shulker box are all block entities in 26.3, so no
   station exists in his capability surface today.

## Notes on the two decisions this table forces

**The `Player` shim is the pivot.** Rows "use item", "use on block", "use on entity", "enchanting", "anvil",
"loom", "grindstone", "cartography" and "shift-click" all reduce to the same missing thing: something that
is a `Player`. Everything else on the list — furnace, brewing stand, stonecutter, smithing, chest, shulker,
merchant, beacon, eating, potions, XP, durability with Unbreaking — is reachable today with a faithful
`ServerPlayer`-free implementation, because the underlying machines are self-ticking block entities or plain
`Container`s and their effects are `LivingEntity`-typed.

That split suggests an ordering: ship the block-entity and container work first (it is mostly plumbing and
it unblocks iron, potions, storage and trading), and treat the shim as a separate, riskier project. If the
shim is built, it should be a never-spawned `ServerPlayer` (no `addFreshEntity`, no chunk ticket, a no-op
connection) purely as an `InteractionHand` context holder — a second visible player entity would break the
"companion, not a second player" premise.

**Hunger was a design question until 0.85.0, and the answer was not to reimplement `tick`.**
`FoodProperties.onConsume` grants nutrition only inside an `instanceof Player` branch, and
`FoodData.tick(ServerPlayer)` drives regeneration and starvation against a player. The way through was to
give the game a player whose bar, health and effects are the companion's (`Hunger.Actor`, a `FakePlayer`
with the inherited `foodData` field set to his `FoodData` and every body question forwarded), so
`FoodData.tick` runs unchanged. Eating and drinking then work through `ItemStack.use` on that actor, and
nothing in the mod knows a food's values.

# Cobbie — World & Block Interaction, Mobs, Farming

Read-only audit against `minecraft-merged-deobf-26.3.jar` (javap) and
`src/main/java/com/rishi/cobbie` at `132d39e70`. No files in the repo were touched.

## The one structural fact that governs this whole area

Every right-click capability in the game is expressed as a method that takes a **`Player`** as the actor:

- `ServerPlayerGameMode.useItemOn(ServerPlayer, Level, ItemStack, InteractionHand, BlockHitResult)` and `useItem(...)` — the whole block/entity right-click chain.
- `BlockStateBase.useItemOn(ItemStack, Level, Player, InteractionHand, BlockHitResult)` / `useWithoutItem(Level, Player, BlockHitResult)` — what chests, furnaces, levers, note blocks, jukeboxes, signs, repeaters all implement.
- `Player.interactOn(Entity, InteractionHand, Vec3)` → `Mob.interact(Player, InteractionHand, Vec3)` → `Mob.mobInteract(Player, InteractionHand)` — shearing, milking, feeding, breeding, taming, trading, name tags.
- `Player.openMenu(MenuProvider)` (it lives on `Player`, not just `ServerPlayer`), `Player.startSleepInBed(AbstractBedBlock, BlockState, BedRule, BlockPos)`, `SignBlock.openTextEdit(Player, SignBlockEntity, SignTextSlot)`.

Cobbie is `CobbieEntity extends PathfinderMob`. A `PathfinderMob` has no `Player` identity, so none of these paths are callable with `this`. Two ways out, and the choice shapes every row below:

1. **Fabric `FakePlayer`** — `net.fabricmc.fabric.api.entity.FakePlayer extends ServerPlayer` ships in `fabric-events-interaction-v0-5.3.7` (already on the compile classpath via `fabric-api-0.162.0+26.3`): `FakePlayer.get(ServerLevel)`. Pass it as the `Player` argument and every vanilla interaction runs unmodified, including menu-based ones (`FakePlayer` overrides `openMenu`, `openTextEdit`, `startSleeping`). Cost: a second "player" exists in the world (stats, `Container.stillValid`, `hasContainerOpen` counters, scoreboard team, `PiglinAi.angerNearbyPiglins`).
2. **Reimplement each interaction against the block/entity API** — direct, no second actor, but each row is hand-written and drifts from vanilla (`Container` reads, `Merchant` offers, `AbstractFurnaceBlockEntity` slots, crop ages).

Today Cobbie does neither. `grep -rn "interactOn\|useItemOn\|gameMode\|openMenu" src/main/java` returns **nothing**. Everything is hand-rolled through `ServerLevel.setBlock` / `ServerLevel.destroyBlock`.

## What a `PathfinderMob` structurally cannot do

- **Be a `Player` actor.** Every `useItemOn`/`useWithoutItem`/`mobInteract`/`openMenu`/`openTextEdit`/`startSleepInBed` call needs one. This is the single blocker behind roughly two-thirds of the rows above.
- **Sleep, set a respawn point, have a bed spawn.** `Player.startSleepInBed` and `ServerPlayer.setRespawnPosition(ServerPlayer.RespawnConfig, boolean)` are `Player`-only, and the sleep result is an `Either<BedSleepingProblem, Unit>` evaluated against a player's respawn anchor.
- **Own a validated container.** `AbstractContainerMenu` is constructed with a `Player` inventory; `Container.stillValid(Player)` is checked on every tick of an open chest. A `SimpleContainer` cannot back one.
- **Trade as a merchant counterparty.** `MerchantMenu` needs a `Player` to hold the input slots and to receive `notifyTrade`.
- **Hold XP, levels or an enchanting budget.** `Player.experienceLevel`, `totalExperience`, `giveExperiencePoints(int)` and `getXpNeededForNextLevel()` live on `Player`; a mob has none of them, so enchanting, anvil repair and villager level-ups have no budget to spend.
- **Have an inventory the game can open.** Anything that offers `MenuProvider` to Cobbie's "inventory" would hand the model a `Player`-typed slot API it cannot fill.

## Where the least code buys the most player-likeness

The rows marked high cluster into three implementations, not thirty: (1) a `FakePlayer` actor plus a single `rightClick(BlockPos)` / `rightClick(Entity)` verb that funnels everything through `ServerPlayerGameMode.useItemOn` and `Player.interactOn`; (2) a `Container` read/write pair for block entities, which needs no `Player` at all and covers chests, barrels, shulkers and furnaces; (3) a real `BlockPlaceContext` in `placeBlock`, which fixes stairs, slabs, doors, beds, signs, torches and chest-facing in one change.
